In short

Most roof leaks in the west of Scotland come from a short list of predictable faults: slipped or cracked slates, failed lead flashing where the roof meets a chimney or wall, blocked or split valleys, and washed-out ridge mortar. A roof repair means finding which of those is actually letting water in and putting it right, rather than sealing over the damp patch and hoping. Most single repairs are a same-day job once we are on site.

A leak almost never shows up directly under the hole. Water runs down the underside of the sarking and along the rafters before it drips, so the stain on your ceiling can be a good distance from the actual fault. That is why finding the cause matters far more than patching the symptom.

Common causes of a leaking roof

  • Slipped, cracked or missing slates and tiles
  • Failed lead flashing where the roof meets a chimney or gable wall
  • Blocked, split or badly formed valleys between two roof slopes
  • Cracked or washed-out mortar along the ridge or at the verge
  • Overflowing gutters pushing water back under the bottom course
  • Perished sarking felt at the eaves letting water past the gutter line

Frequently asked questions

How quickly can you look at a leak?

Call or WhatsApp 07464 695657 and we will tell you honestly how soon we can get to you. Active leaks in wet weather are treated as urgent, because water sitting in a ceiling causes far more damage than the fault that let it in.

Can you repair a roof in the rain?

A temporary cover can usually go on straight away to stop water getting in, but permanent work such as bedding mortar or dressing lead needs dry conditions to be done properly. In Scotland that often means working around the forecast rather than against it.

Is it worth repairing an old roof?

If the covering is sound and the problem is in one area, a repair is much better value. Once slates are slipping in several different places and the sarking underneath has broken down, repeated repairs cost more over a few years than replacing the roof once.

Do you find the leak before quoting?

Yes. Quoting for a roof leak without establishing where the water is actually getting in is guesswork, and it is how people end up paying for work that does not fix the problem.

Why does my roof only leak in driving rain?

That usually points to a fault that only lets water past at a certain angle or volume, such as a gap under a flashing, a hairline crack in a slate, or a valley that overtops when it fills. It is a real fault and it will get worse, even though the roof seems fine most of the time.
